Forty runs, no retraining

Every model this project has trained left its validation posteriors on disk. Averaging them costs no GPU at all — and it works for video and fails for EEG, which turns out to say something about each modality.

Video uses the 40 runs in output/v3_vidseeds (10 backbones × 4 seeds, 5,289 validation clips). EEG uses the 35 in output/v3_bestcfg (7 architectures × 5 seeds, 5,319 clips). Ensembling is the unweighted mean of member posteriors; metrics are macro-averaged, matching the project’s tables.

01 — The asymmetry

Averaging helps one modality and hurts the other

For video, the ensemble beats the best single run at all three tasks. For EEG it loses at all three — and the gap widens as the task gets harder.

02 — Why

A tight pool averages well; a ragged one averages to its middle

The video backbones all land close together — at detection the whole family spans 0.943 to 0.976. Averaging them finds a consensus better than any member.

The EEG pool is nothing like that. At 3-class its members run from 0.227 to 0.777: strong recurrent models beside classical baselines and a few runs that collapsed outright. An unweighted mean is dragged toward the middle of that distribution, and the middle is well below the top.

03 — Per backbone

Seed-averaging one backbone captures most of it

You do not need all forty models at inference. Averaging the four seeds of a single video backbone recovers a large share of the gain at a tenth of the cost — SlowFast reaches 0.687 at 5-class against the full ensemble’s 0.698.

04 — Nothing rescues EEG

Curating the pool does not help either

If weak members are the problem, removing them should fix it. Four selection rules were tried — two of which are honest (they use only architecture family and each run’s own recorded training history) and two of which cheat, selecting on the very test scores being reported.

Even the cheating rules only bring the EEG ensemble level with the best single model. There is no configuration in which averaging EEG models is the right move.

05 — The explanation that failed

It is not that EEG models make the same mistakes

The natural story is that video members err on different clips, so averaging fixes them, while EEG members all fail on the same clips, so averaging cannot. It is a good story and the data does not support it.

At 5-class the irreducible share is 2.3% for EEG and 2.2% for video — indistinguishable. On severe clips specifically, EEG is more recoverable in principle: 13 of 262 missed by everything (5.0%) against video’s 29 of 201 (14.6%).

So EEG errors are diverse, yet averaging still fails to convert that diversity into a better decision. The pool-quality account above fits the numbers; the error-correlation account does not. I can tell you the effect is real and reproducible. I cannot tell you the mechanism.

06 — What to do

Practical consequences

  • Ensemble the video runs. +0.003 / +0.011 / +0.014 macro-F1 at detection / 3-class / 5-class, for nothing. Curation is unnecessary — average all forty.
  • If inference cost matters, seed-average one backbone. Four models instead of forty recovers most of the gain.
  • Do not ensemble the EEG models. Ship the best single TCN run. Averaging costs you 0.007 to 0.020 macro-F1.
  • Do not compare the two columns directly. The validation sets differ and overlap by only 2,830 of ~5,300 clips.

This bears on a paper claim. Table 1 states that EEG edges video at detection by 0.006 macro-F1 (Welch p = 0.003). Video ensembling takes video detection to 0.9792 against EEG’s best single 0.9793 — the margin disappears.

But those two numbers come from different clip sets, so neither the original claim nor its reversal is established. The comparison has to be recomputed on the 2,830-clip intersection before anyone cites it in either direction.

Three things that changed the numbers. Nine of forty video runs per task store their rows in a different order, so members must be aligned on the clip path — averaging by row index mixes probability vectors across clips and inflates detection to 0.986. Four video runs per task store double-softmaxed posteriors and need inverting first. And the metrics here come from an isolated virtual environment rather than the shared base interpreter, which the project’s own environment file warns against installing into.

Video: 40 runs, 10 backbones × 4 seeds, 5,289 clips. EEG: 35 runs, 7 architectures × 5 seeds, 5,319 clips.
